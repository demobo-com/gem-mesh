Your data, your devices, your rules. Every gem is a node, and your devices are the mesh. Open infrastructure for private AI apps: your phone, your laptop and your office GPUs act as one. Every request is checked on your own device and sent to the smallest model that can answer it. Nothing leaves your hardware unless you say so.

The problem

Anyone who wants a private assistant is stuck between two bad options

Option A

Cloud assistant

Convenient everywhere. But every client name, salary and case file lands on someone else's servers, under their terms and their jurisdiction.

Option B

One local model

Private. But slow on a phone, gone when the laptop closes, and too small for real work.

The mesh

Data sovereignty, at full strength

Your own devices as tiers: small model on the phone, bigger on the laptop, biggest in the office. You own the hardware and the rules. A frontier model stays an option, per request, for public text only. One assistant, one memory, one rule about where text may go.

Who: anyone whose conversations are their own business. Professionals with client data, people with a journal or a companion, families, developers.

How a request moves

Decided on your device, before any text leaves it

1

Rules

Keys, emails, paths, your own terms. A hit is sensitive, no appeal.

2

Local classifier

Gemma 4 on the phone or laptop labels public or sensitive. Unsure counts as sensitive.

3

Tier window

Difficulty sets the floor. Long or hard work prefers the effort tier. Sensitivity caps the ceiling.

4

Reachable nodes

Live table from discovery on Wi-Fi or hotspot. Lowest tier that fits wins.

Run on the chosen node Queue until a node returns Ask approval for the frontier

Every answer carries a badge: which device, which model, whether anything left the mesh. Web search works in any conversation; only the query leaves, and it is checked first. Conversations themselves never leave for an outside model. Rules, classifier prompt and tier policy are files you edit.

Data sovereignty

Your data stays on hardware you own, under rules you set.

Memory lives only in the user's own phone and laptop. Shared machines compute and remember nothing. The only path out is text the user approved. Once paired, devices talk only to each other, encrypted, and a stranger's node on the same Wi-Fi gets nothing. Pairs never talk to each other; remove servers or the frontier and nothing else changes.

Infrastructure, not an app

Gem Mesh is the framework. You build the app.

Example app · Work

A client-safe assistant

A firm builds it for its lawyers or HR team. Its rule file names clients and matters, so their text never leaves the firm's devices. Documents in, memos and PDFs out, each change approved.

Example app · Companion

A private companion

A developer builds it for one person. It lives on the phone, remembers what it is told, and keeps photos and conversations on that person's devices; it is sharper when their Mac is near.

The mesh gives every app the same things: routing across devices, the privacy check, memory, the queue, tools with approval. An app adds its own policy file, persona and screens.

POC on hardware we already have

A Pixel 11 and a MacBook, nothing else

Mesh

Node server and discovery

OpenAI-compatible server around LiteRT-LM with session KV reuse, advertising itself over mDNS with tier and load.

Thin clients

Router, memory, queue

Rules, local classifier, tier window, network detected automatically. Memory remembers facts from plain chat, held on the mobile side and mirrored to the laptop. Tasks queue until a tier that fits returns. Web search, a demo file folder, /think and an approval-gated /frontier.

Actions

Files and photos, on your devices

The chats create and edit text, PDF and image files: memos, stamps, page edits, captions, collages. The phone's E4B can see and hear: photos are judged and fixed on the device, their GPS location removed before sharing, and speech is typed for you without leaving the phone. Every change is shown and approved; the old version is kept.

The demo runs on the pair alone; a workstation is a bonus tier, not a dependency. Until the Pixel app lands, developers use a phone stand-in on the Mac (./install.sh --dev). One ./install.sh sizes the models to any Apple silicon Mac, from E2B on 8 GB to the 26B on 32 GB and up.

Open source · try it yourself

  
Clone, install, talk to it

  

    
On your Mac Apple silicon, 8 GB or more

    

      
1
Clone
git clone https://github.com/demobo-com/gem-mesh gem-mesh
Then cd gem-mesh

      
2
Install
./install.sh
Picks the Gemma 4 model that fits your Mac (E2B to the 26B) and runs it as a mesh node your phone uses.

      
3
Talk to it
gem-mesh
Try: Brighten the Milo photos, crop them square and take the location off.

      
4
Pair a phone
gem-mesh pair
Shows a QR code for the Android app.

    

    
On your Android phone in development · 8 GB or more

    

      
1
Install the app
Download the APK from the project's GitHub Releases page and open it.

      
2
Open it once on Wi-Fi
It downloads Gemma 4 E4B (3.7 GB) to run on the phone.

      
3
Scan the QR
From gem-mesh pair. On the same Wi-Fi the phone usually finds the Mac by itself.

    

    
Optional: a bigger node M5 Ultra Mac Studio, Linux with NVIDIA, or any spare Mac

    

      
1
Clone
The same repository, on that machine.

      
2
Install as a node
./install.sh --role workstation
Picks the biggest Gemma 4 that fits: the 31B on a Mac Studio or an RTX 5090. Already installed? gem-mesh role workstation

      
3
Nothing else
The MacBook and phone find it; gem-mesh nodes lists it. Paired mesh: gem-mesh join with the Mac's code.
